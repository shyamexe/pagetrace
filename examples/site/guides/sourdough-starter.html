How to keep a sourdough starter alive

A starter needs feeding once a day at room temperature, or once a week if you keep it in the fridge, and the ratio matters more than the schedule does.

Feeding ratio

Equal weights of starter, flour and water is the simplest place to begin. If your kitchen runs warm, drop the starter to a fifth of the flour weight and it will still be ready in twelve hours rather than souring on you.

Signs it is healthy

A healthy starter roughly doubles within six hours of feeding, smells like yoghurt rather than acetone, and floats when you drop a spoonful into water. Acetone means it is hungry, not dead.

Reviving a neglected starter

A starter left for months in the fridge is usually recoverable. Pour off the dark liquid on top, keep a spoonful of what is underneath, and feed it twice a day at room temperature. It should be rising predictably again within three or four days, and if it is not by the end of a week, start over.
